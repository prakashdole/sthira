*(Proactive Uninhabitable-Zone Network Assessment & Relocation Viability Analysis System)*

## 1. System Vision & Unified Operational Philosophy

The platform operates on a **Dual-Horizon Architecture**:

1. **Strategic Planning Horizon (Primary SIH Mandate):** A long-term Spatial Decision Support System (SDSS) for State Disaster Management Authorities (SDMAs). It automates dynamic multi-hazard Red-Zoning, evaluates multi-vector Carrying Capacity for resettlement sites, and prioritizes habitations into phased relocation queues (*Immediate: 0–6 months*, *Short-Term: 1–2 years*, *Medium-Term: 3–5 years*).
2. **Tactical Operational Horizon ("God's View" Command HUD):** A real-time situational awareness dashboard (World Monitor paradigm) designed for District Emergency Operation Centers (EOCs). While settlements await permanent relocation, this mode continuously monitors live seismic, rainfall, and ground-deformation telemetry, executes dynamic road blockage rerouting (OSRM), computes instantaneous logistics/supply needs, and provides a hands-free Indic voice interface powered by Sarvam AI.

## 3. Mathematical Formulations & Algorithmic Engine

### 3.1. Hazard-Based Red-Zone Delineation

A raster parcel ($10\text{m}\times 10\text{m}$) is assigned to the **Red Zone** if the physical **Factor of Safety ($F_s$)** drops below critical stability, or if satellite radar measures ongoing catastrophic creep:

$$F_s = \frac{c' + (\gamma_{\text{sat}} \cdot z - \gamma_w \cdot h_w) \cos^2\beta \tan\phi'}{\gamma_{\text{sat}} \cdot z \cdot \sin\beta \cos\beta}$$

Where:

* $c'$ = Effective soil cohesion ($\text{kPa}$) from Geological Survey of India (GSI) lithology profiles.
* $\gamma_{\text{sat}}$ = Saturated unit weight of soil ($\text{kN/m}^3$).
* $\gamma_w$ = Unit weight of water ($9.81\text{ kN/m}^3$).
* $z$ = Soil mantle depth ($\text{m}$) from CartoDEM digital depth models.
* $h_w$ = Water table height above slip surface ($\text{m}$), driven dynamically by cumulative 72-hour IMD rainfall.
* $\beta$ = Slope angle in degrees ($\beta = \arctan(\sqrt{(\frac{\partial z}{\partial x})^2 + (\frac{\partial z}{\partial y})^2})$).
* $\phi'$ = Effective internal angle of friction.

**Multi-Hazard InSAR Red-Zone Condition:**


$$\text{Zone Classification} = \begin{cases}  \mathbf{RED} & \text{if } F_s < 1.0 \quad \text{OR} \quad \left\vert{}\frac{d_{\text{InSAR}}}{dt}\right\vert{} > 15\text{ mm/year} \quad \text{OR} \quad \text{FloodDepth}_{100\text{yr}} > 2.0\text{ m} \\ \mathbf{ORANGE} & \text{if } 1.0 \le F_s \le 1.3 \quad \text{OR} \quad 5\text{ mm/year} < \left\vert{}\frac{d_{\text{InSAR}}}{dt}\right\vert{} \le 15\text{ mm/year} \\ \mathbf{GREEN} & \text{Otherwise} \end{cases}$$

### 3.2. Dasymetric High-Resolution Population Exposure

To calculate populations in scattered remote habitations where only aggregate Census data exists:

```
                  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │ Village Total Census Population: P_village               │
                  └────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┘
                                               │
                                               ▼
                  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │ Extract Microsoft/Google Building Footprints: {B_1...B_N}│
                  │ Calculate Footprint Planar Areas: a_i = ST_Area(B_i)     │
                  └────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┘
                                               │
                                               ▼
                  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │ Compute Spatial Intersection with Red Zone Polygon:      │
                  │               B_in_danger = B_i ∩ Poly_Red               │
                  └────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┘
                                               │
                                               ▼
                  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │ Affected Population Formula:                             │
                  │   P_affected = P_village * ( Σ a_k / Σ a_i )             │
                  │   where k ∈ B_in_danger, i ∈ Total Village               │
                  └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

```

$$P_{\text{affected}} = P_{\text{village}} \times \left( \frac{\sum_{k \in \mathcal{B}_{\text{Red}}} \text{Area}(B_k)}{\sum_{i=1}^{N} \text{Area}(B_i)} \right)$$

### 3.3. Multi-Vector Carrying Capacity Score ($CC_{\text{score}}$)

A candidate resettlement site ($S$) must satisfy ecological, infrastructural, hydrological, and livelihood safety bounds. If any non-negotiable threshold is breached (e.g., inside a Reserve Forest or mean slope $>20^\circ$), the site is immediately disqualified ($CC = 0$).

For legal sites, compute the weighted normalized vector:

$$CC_{\text{score}}(S) = w_t \cdot T(S) + w_h \cdot H(S) + w_i \cdot I(S) + w_l \cdot L(S)$$

*Weights constraint:* $\sum w = 1.0$ ($w_t=0.25, w_h=0.30, w_i=0.20, w_l=0.25$)

1. **Topographical Factor ($T$):**

$$T(S) = \frac{\text{Contiguous Area with Slope } < 15^\circ}{\text{Gross Site Area}}$$


2. **Hydrological Factor ($H$):** Evaluated against standard rural consumption norms ($70\text{ Liters Per Capita per Day - LPCD}$):

$$H(S) = \min\left(1.0, \, \frac{\text{Aquifer Discharge Rate (liters/day)}}{P_{\text{relocated}} \times 70}\right)$$


3. **Infrastructural Proximity ($I$):** Distance penalty function against arterial corridors:

$$I(S) = \exp\left(-\frac{D_{\text{road}}}{10\text{ km}}\right) \times 0.5 + \exp\left(-\frac{D_{\text{power}}}{5\text{ km}}\right) \times 0.3 + \exp\left(-\frac{D_{\text{phc}}}{12\text{ km}}\right) \times 0.2$$


4. **Livelihood Compatibility Factor ($L$):** Soil capability class match ($C_{\text{soil}}$) and availability of grazing commons ($A_{\text{commons}}$):

$$L(S) = 0.6 \cdot \mathbb{I}(\text{Soil Match}) + 0.4 \cdot \min\left(1.0, \frac{A_{\text{commons}}}{P_{\text{relocated}} \times 0.05\text{ ha}}\right)$$



---

### 3.4. Relocation Phasing Prioritization Metric ($PPM$)

The urgency score ($0\text{ to }100$) determining whether a village goes into Phase 1 (Immediate), Phase 2, or Phase 3:

$$PPM = 0.40 \cdot \mathcal{H}_{\text{intensity}} + 0.25 \cdot \left(\frac{P_{\text{affected}}}{\text{Capacity}_{\text{max}}}\right) + 0.20 \cdot (1.0 - \mathcal{A}_{\text{access}}) + 0.15 \cdot \mathcal{V}_{\text{structural}}$$

Where:

* $\mathcal{H}_{\text{intensity}}$: Normalized Hazard Exceedance ($1 - F_s$ or subsidence velocity).
* $\mathcal{A}_{\text{access}}$: Graph connectivity index ($1.0$ = multiple all-weather highways, $0.0$ = single unpaved trail prone to blockage).
* $\mathcal{V}_{\text{structural}}$: Proportion of non-engineered Kutcha/masonry structures (derived from Census PCA).

$$\text{Action Queue} = \begin{cases}  \mathbf{Phase\ 1:\ Immediate\ (0\text{–}6\ Months)} & \text{if } PPM \ge 75 \\ \mathbf{Phase\ 2:\ Short\text{-}Term\ (1\text{–}2\ Years)} & \text{if } 50 \le PPM < 75 \\ \mathbf{Phase\ 3:\ Medium\text{-}Term\ (3\text{–}5\ Years)} & \text{if } PPM < 50  \end{cases}$$

## 4. Backend Processing Pipelines (FastAPI, PostGIS & Celery)

### 4.1. Real-Time Telemetry Poller & Hazard Worker (`hazard_worker.py`)

This worker runs asynchronously, ingesting sub-minute earthquake records from the National Center for Seismology (NCS) / USGS and high-resolution precipitation data.

```python
import asyncio
import httpx
from datetime import datetime, timezone
import asyncpg

DATABASE_URL = "postgresql://punarvas_admin:secure_pass@localhost:5432/punarvas_db"
USGS_SEISMIC_FEED = "https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/feed/v1.0/summary/all_hour.geojson"

async def process_incoming_seismic_telemetry():
    """Polls real-time feeds, filters for South Asian bounds, and writes to PostGIS."""
    async with httpx.AsyncClient(timeout=10.0) as client:
        response = await client.get(USGS_SEISMIC_FEED)
        data = response.json()

    conn = await asyncpg.connect(DATABASE_URL)
    try:
        for feature in data.get("features", []):
            coords = feature["geometry"]["coordinates"]
            lon, lat, depth = coords[0], coords[1], coords[2]
            props = feature["properties"]
            mag = props["mag"]

            # Filter for Indian Subcontinent Geographic Bounding Box
            if 6.0 <= lat <= 38.0 and 68.0 <= lon <= 98.0 and mag is not None and mag >= 3.5:
                event_id = feature["id"]
                recorded_at = datetime.fromtimestamp(props["time"] / 1000.0, tz=timezone.utc)

                # Upsert into PostGIS
                query = """
                INSERT INTO tactical_telemetry (
                    event_source, external_event_id, magnitude, intensity_metric, location, recorded_at, event_payload
                ) VALUES (
                    'SEISMIC_FEED', $1, $2, 'RICHTER', ST_SetSRID(ST_MakePoint($3, $4), 4326), $5, $6
                )
                ON CONFLICT (external_event_id) DO NOTHING;
                """
                await conn.execute(query, event_id, float(mag), lon, lat, recorded_at, str(props))
                
                # Check for direct red-zone triggering
                if mag >= 5.0:
                    await evaluate_seismic_red_zone_impact(conn, lon, lat, mag)
    finally:
        await conn.close()

async def evaluate_seismic_red_zone_impact(conn, lon: float, lat: float, mag: float):
    """If magnitude exceeds threshold, dynamically expand red zones based on soil amplification."""
    impact_radius_km = (10 ** (0.5 * mag)) / 10.0  # Empirical attenuation radius
    update_query = """
    INSERT INTO hazard_red_zones (hazard_type, severity_level, zone_geometry)
    SELECT 
        'SEISMIC_INDUCED_UNSTABLE', 
        'RED_UNINHABITABLE',
        ST_Multi(ST_Buffer(ST_SetSRID(ST_MakePoint($1, $2), 4326)::geography, $3 * 1000)::geometry)
    """
    await conn.execute(update_query, lon, lat, impact_radius_km)
```

### 4.2. Carrying Capacity & Relocation Matching Service (`capacity_service.py`)

Calculates whether a designated village can be safely absorbed into an alternative site without environmental collapse.

```python
from pydantic import BaseModel
from typing import List, Optional
import asyncpg

class RelocationMatchResult(BaseModel):
    site_id: str
    site_code: str
    carrying_capacity_score: float
    max_sustainable_population: int
    water_adequacy_ratio: float
    is_legally_cleared: bool
    distance_km: float

async def match_safe_resettlement_sites(
    habitation_id: str,
    search_radius_km: float = 35.0,
    db_pool: asyncpg.Pool = None
) -> List[RelocationMatchResult]:
    """
    Queries candidate safe zones outside all red polygons, executes carrying capacity scoring,
    and returns ranked legal sites.
    """
    query = """
    WITH target_hab AS (
        SELECT id, total_population, dominant_livelihood, centroid 
        FROM habitations WHERE id = $1
    )
    SELECT 
        s.id AS site_id,
        s.site_code,
        s.composite_carrying_capacity_score,
        s.max_population_capacity,
        s.groundwater_yield_lps,
        s.is_legally_viable,
        ST_Distance(s.site_geometry::geography, h.centroid::geography) / 1000.0 AS distance_km,
        (s.groundwater_yield_lps * 86400.0) / (h.total_population * 70.0) AS water_adequacy_ratio
    FROM candidate_relocation_sites s, target_hab h
    WHERE ST_DWithin(s.site_geometry::geography, h.centroid::geography, $2 * 1000.0)
      AND s.is_legally_viable = TRUE
      AND NOT EXISTS (
          SELECT 1 FROM hazard_red_zones r 
          WHERE ST_Intersects(s.site_geometry, r.zone_geometry)
            AND r.severity_level = 'RED_UNINHABITABLE'
      )
    ORDER BY s.composite_carrying_capacity_score DESC, distance_km ASC
    LIMIT 5;
    """
    async with db_pool.acquire() as conn:
        rows = await conn.fetch(query, habitation_id, search_radius_km)
        return [
            RelocationMatchResult(
                site_id=str(r["site_id"]),
                site_code=r["site_code"],
                carrying_capacity_score=float(r["composite_carrying_capacity_score"]),
                max_sustainable_population=r["max_population_capacity"],
                water_adequacy_ratio=float(r["water_adequacy_ratio"]),
                is_legally_cleared=r["is_legally_viable"],
                distance_km=float(r["distance_km"])
            )
            for r in rows
        ]

```

### 4.3. Dynamic Road Obstruction & Emergency Logistics Routing (`tactical_router.py`)

Detects severed road connections intersecting newly formed Red Zones and computes dynamic detours using `pgrouting` or OSRM.

```python
import asyncpg
from typing import Dict, Any

async def compute_tactical_corridor_and_supplies(
    village_id: str,
    db_pool: asyncpg.Pool
) -> Dict[str, Any]:
    """
    1. Checks if all ground corridors to the village are severed by Red Zones.
    2. Computes the shortest surviving terrestrial aid route.
    3. If fully isolated, flags AIR_EXTRACTION_REQUIRED and estimates immediate supply tons.
    """
    async with db_pool.acquire() as conn:
        # Check road severance
        severance_query = """
        SELECT COUNT(e.id) as blocked_edges
        FROM transport_network e
        JOIN hazard_red_zones r ON ST_Intersects(e.the_geom, r.zone_geometry)
        JOIN habitations h ON ST_DWithin(e.the_geom::geography, h.centroid::geography, 5000)
        WHERE h.id = $1 AND r.severity_level = 'RED_UNINHABITABLE';
        """
        blocked_count = await conn.fetchval(severance_query, village_id)

        # Retrieve population metrics for logistics calculation
        hab_data = await conn.fetchrow(
            "SELECT total_population, total_households FROM habitations WHERE id = $1", 
            village_id
        )
        pop = hab_data["total_population"]
        households = hab_data["total_households"]

        # Calculate NDMA 72-hour survival logistics
        potable_water_liters_72h = pop * 4.0 * 3        # 4 Liters/day/person
        food_ration_packets_72h = pop * 3              # 3 Meals/day/person
        emergency_shelter_tents = int(households * 1.1) # 1 tent per family + 10% reserve
        first_aid_trauma_kits = int(pop * 0.15)

        total_payload_kg = (potable_water_liters_72h * 1.0) + (food_ration_packets_72h * 0.8) + (emergency_shelter_tents * 14.0)

        is_airdrop_only = blocked_count > 0  # Trigger if local access roads are cut

        return {
            "habitation_id": village_id,
            "population_at_risk": pop,
            "terrestrial_access_compromised": is_airdrop_only,
            "evacuation_mode": "AIR_ROTARY_WING" if is_airdrop_only else "CONVOY_TERRESTRIAL",
            "supplies_required_72h": {
                "potable_water_liters": potable_water_liters_72h,
                "food_ration_packets": food_ration_packets_72h,
                "shelter_tents": emergency_shelter_tents,
                "trauma_kits": first_aid_trauma_kits,
                "gross_weight_metric_tons": round(total_payload_kg / 1000.0, 2)
            }
        }

```

Your skepticism is completely justified: if a system relied purely on optical satellite photos to declare an area safe or dangerous, it would fail in the real world. Optical satellites cannot see through monsoon clouds, and a flat patch of "green" on a satellite image could easily be a waterlogged peat bog, private disputed farmland, or a legally protected reserve forest.

Here is how real-world geospatial systems, sensor networks, and data pipelines actually solve every blind spot you just raised.

---

### 1. Which Open-Source Satellites We Use (And How They See Through Clouds)

You cannot rely on regular optical satellite photography (like Google Earth or Sentinel-2) during disaster seasons because monsoons bring 100% cloud cover. Instead, earth observation relies on **radar**:

* **Copernicus Sentinel-1 (C-Band Synthetic Aperture Radar - SAR):**
* **How it works:** It does not take a photo. It emits active microwave radar pulses (wavelength ~5.6 cm) that punch straight through clouds, rain, smoke, and darkness, bouncing off the bedrock.
* **Precision:** By comparing two radar passes 6 to 12 days apart using **InSAR (Interferometric SAR)**, algorithms calculate ground displacement down to **3 to 5 millimeters**.
* **Cost & Access:** 100% free and open-access via the European Space Agency (ESA) Copernicus SciHub.


* **Copernicus DEM & CartoDEM (ISRO):**
* Provides a 30-meter elevation grid. It does not matter what color the surface is; it gives you the mathematical slope angle ($\beta$), terrain curvature, and water runoff accumulation paths.


* **Sentinel-2 & Landsat 8/9 (Multispectral Optical - for dry seasons):**
* Used outside monsoon periods. They measure non-visible light spectrums: Near-Infrared (NIR) and Short-Wave Infrared (SWIR) to assess soil moisture, clay content, and vegetation root health.



---

### 2. How to Flag a Red Zone When Satellites Miss It

Satellites have a revisit latency of 5 to 12 days. If an active slope starts cracking on a Tuesday, a satellite radar pass might not process until next Monday. You need **secondary and tertiary ground triggers**:

```
 ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │                            3-TIER INGESTION PIPELINE                       │
 ├────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ TIER 1: Satellite InSAR & DEM  │ 6-12 Day Revisit: Detects slow, regional  │
 │ (Physics/Topography)           │ ground subsidence (mm/year) and slopes.   │
 ├────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ TIER 2: Vernacular NLP & News  │ Minutes to Hours: Scrapes local reports of│
 │ (Social / Micro-News Engine)   │ "cracks in walls", "muddy spring water".  │
 ├────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ TIER 3: Official Ground Assets │ Real-Time: Patwari reports, Gram Panchayat│
 │ (Participatory Verification)   │ distress logs via mobile GIS forms.       │
 └────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────┘

```

#### The Vernacular NLP Trigger (News & Micro-Reports)

Disasters almost always show hyper-local human warning signs days before a collapse:

1. Cracks appearing in masonry houses (like Joshimath).
2. Natural mountain springs suddenly discharging muddy/turbid water (indicates shear failure inside the hill's water table).
3. Minor retaining wall bulges along state highways.

**How the platform captures this:**

* Local regional news portals (Dainik Jagran, Amar Ujala, local regional language RSS feeds) and district Twitter/X feeds are scraped continuously.
* A lightweight Named Entity Recognition (NER) model (such as fine-tuned IndicBERT) extracts:
* **Location Entities:** Village, Taluk, or Landmark names (e.g., *"Helang"*, *"Sunil Ward"*).
* **Disaster Token Dictionary:** Words denoting structural failure (*"दरारें"* / cracks, *"भू-धंसाव"* / subsidence, *"मलबा"* / debris).


* **The Fusion Logic:** If the NLP model detects an unverified micro-report of cracks in Village X, the platform does not blindly mark it "Red." Instead, it creates an **Urgent Watch Polygon** in the PostGIS database, which immediately pulls that village's elevation and slope profile. If the village sits on a $>30^\circ$ slope, the system flags an immediate alert for ground verification.

### 3. How the "Base AI" Fuses All This Data Without Breaking Down

A common misconception is that one giant "black-box" AI takes satellite rasters, tweets, weather data, and maps all at once and magically spits out answers. That approach is fragile and un-debuggable.

Instead, the system uses a **Modular Geospatial Stack**:

```
[ Unstructured Inputs ]          [ Spatial Deterministic Layers ]
News / Tweets / Crowdsource      DEM Elevation / Faults / Rainfall
           │                                      │
           ▼                                      ▼
┌───────────────────────┐              ┌───────────────────────┐
│ NLP Information Extr. │              │ Physics & GIS Engine  │
│ (BERT / IndicBERT)    │              │ (GDAL / PostGIS / GRASS)
│ Extracts LGD Codes &  │              │ Calculates Slope, Flow│
│ Distress Severity     │              │ Acc., Factor of Safety│
└──────────┬────────────┘              └──────────┬────────────┘
           │                                      │
           └───────────────────┬──────────────────┘
                               ▼
            ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
            │   Machine Learning Scoring Engine    │
            │  (Random Forest / XGBoost Classifier)│
            │ Inputs: Soil, Slope, InSAR, Rain,    │
            │         Historical slide inventory   │
            │ Output: Susceptibility Index (0-100) │
            └──────────────────┬───────────────────┘
                               ▼
                     [ Dynamic Red Zone ]

```

1. **Deterministic Layer (Physics & Topography):** Handled by PostGIS and GDAL. Calculates fixed physical laws: terrain slope, water flow direction, drainage accumulation, and proximity to tectonic fault lines. This does not require AI; it uses geometric and hydrologic formulas.
2. **Predictive Machine Learning Layer (Susceptibility):** An **XGBoost or Random Forest** model trained on the Geological Survey of India’s (GSI) historical landslide inventory. It looks at the combined vectors (Slope + Bedrock Lithology + Soil Type + 72-hour Rainfall Runoff) and outputs a probability score from $0.0$ to $1.0$.
3. **Trigger Layer (Live Telemetry & NLP):** Updates the probability score in real time when new rainfall data, seismic shocks, or local news reports cross established thresholds.

### 4. Solving the "Green Ground" Problem: How to Evaluate Land Remotely

A satellite image showing green flat land means very little on its own. To determine whether a piece of land can actually support relocated human beings, the platform applies **four deterministic filters** before an algorithm ever ranks it:

#### Filter 1: Legal & Cadastral Feasibility (The Non-Negotiable Filter)

* **The Problem:** The land is flat and empty, but it is a legally protected Tiger Reserve or National Park. Moving people there violates the Forest Conservation Act of 1980 and will be stopped by courts immediately.
* **The Solution:** Cross-reference candidate parcels against **ISRO Bhuvan Cadastral Boundaries** and state revenue records:
* *Forest Land / Eco-Sensitive Zones (ESZ):* Hard disqualification ($CC = 0$).
* *Private Agricultural Land:* Flags high land acquisition costs and legal delays.
* *Government Waste Land / Revenue Land (Gair Mumkin):* Preferred legal candidate.



#### Filter 2: Hydrological & Groundwater Reality

* **The Problem:** The site looks dry and usable, but there is no drinking water, or it is a seasonal swamp that floods during peak monsoons.
* **The Solution:**
* **Dry Season vs. Wet Season Multispectral Indexing:** Compute the Normalized Difference Water Index (NDWI) using historical satellite archives. If NDWI spikes during rains, the parcel is a seasonal wetland and is rejected.
* **Aquifer Depth & Yield:** Ingest open data from the **Central Ground Water Board (CGWB) National Aquifer Mapping Program (NAQUIM)**. The database knows whether a specific micro-watershed has an average borehole discharge rate sufficient to provide 70 liters per person per day.



#### Filter 3: Soil Bearing Capacity & Foundation Safety

* **The Problem:** The land is flat, but the soil is loose river silt that will fail or liquefy during moderate tremors.
* **The Solution:** Intersect the parcel with Geological Survey of India (GSI) **1:50,000 Lithology Maps**. The system checks parent bedrock material:
* *Granite, Gneiss, Hard Sandstone:* High bearing capacity $\rightarrow$ Safe for foundations.
* *Alluvial Silt, Unconsolidated Scree, Peat:* Disqualified or penalized.

### 5. The Critical Engineering Boundary: Decision Support vs. Field Execution

No software platform can, or should, order an entire village to be bulldozed and relocated without human verification.

The purpose of this platform is **not to replace the field inspection, but to eliminate 95% of the blind searching**.

* **Without this platform:** After a disaster, government bureaucrats spend 12 to 18 months manually debating where to acquire land, often picking poor locations that are legally contested or hydrologically dry.
* **With this platform:** In 10 seconds, the system filters a 500-square-kilometer district down to the **top 3 legally viable, hydrologically sound, geotechnically stable parcels** within a 15 km radius.

The software then outputs an **Automated Field Audit Checklist** for the District Collector's engineers:

> *"Parcel SITE-B-04 cleared all preliminary filters. Field verification team required to conduct: (1) Standard Penetration Test (SPT) at coordinates $30.54^\circ\text{N}, 79.52^\circ\text{E}$, (2) Perennial spring discharge audit, (3) Gram Sabha consultation under PESA Act."*

This is why it is called a **Spatial Decision Support System (SDSS)**. It takes weeks of fragmented satellite, geological, legal, and news data, runs the physical and legal constraints automatically, and hands officials clear, verifiable options.

This exact scenario is what broke disaster response during the **2013 Kedarnath floods** and the **2021 Chamoli disaster**: small, unmapped habitations (*thoks* or *tolas*) in narrow Himalayan valleys were cut off from road access, with zero official records of how many people were stranded or whether anyone was still alive.

Real-world disaster platforms and geospatial engines solve this through **Structural Remote Sensing** for counting, and **Topological Escape Routing & Aerial Corridor Mapping** for rescue.

### Part 1: How to Count People Where No Census Official Has Gone

Even if a government census worker never hiked to a remote mountain hamlet, humans leave physical signatures on the surface of the Earth. You do not count "people"; you count **habitable physical infrastructure** from space and multiply it by validated regional multipliers.

```
       [ High-Res Satellite Imagery (0.5m) ]
                         │
                         ▼
       [ Building Footprint AI Extraction ]
  (Detects distinct tin, slate, or stone roofs)
                         │
                         ▼
       [ Dwelling Classification & Validation ]
  • Filter out cattle sheds / barns by area (< 15 sq m)
  • Detect active human life (solar panels, trails, smoke)
                         │
                         ▼
       [ Regional Household Multiplier Applied ]
   Occupancy = Validated Dwellings × Regional Multiplier (e.g., 4.6)

```

#### 1. Building Footprint Extraction via Computer Vision

* Datasets like **Google Open Buildings** and **Microsoft Building Footprints** map individual building roofs across India using sub-meter satellite imagery.
* If 18 structures are clustered on a mountain ridge 30 km from the nearest road, computer vision models outline every single roof polygon, calculating its square area, orientation, and perimeter.

#### 2. Differentiating Homes from Cattle Sheds (*Chhans*)

In Uttarakhand, mountain families often have separate stone shelters for livestock (*chhans*) or hay storage. A raw building count will overestimate the human population. The platform filters them out using geometric and thermal filters:

* **Footprint Area Threshold:** Living quarters in rural Himalayan terrain generally exceed $25\text{ to }30\text{ m}^2$. Structures under $15\text{ m}^2$ are classified as storage, latrines, or animal pens.
* **Trail Network Density:** Inhabited dwellings are connected by high-contrast footpath lines (*pagdandis*) visible on high-resolution multispectral imagery.

#### 3. The Occupancy Multiplier

Take the count of verified residential structures and apply the **Census District Household Multiplier**:


$$\text{Estimated Remote Population} = N_{\text{Residential Dwellings}} \times \mu_{\text{District Household Size}}$$

* For remote rural Chamoli or Pithoragarh, the state census provides a baseline of $\approx 4.4\text{ to }4.8\text{ persons per household}$.
* If the system detects 22 residential structures, the estimated population is immediately pegged at $\approx 95\text{ to }105\text{ people}$. This gives first responders an immediate baseline for required supplies.

#### 4. The Administrative Digital Footprint (Proxy Datasets)

Even the most isolated village touches state systems in subtle ways:

* **PMGSY Habitation Master:** The Ministry of Rural Development maintains GPS coordinates for all habitations, even those with under 100 people, to plan future footbridges or bridle paths.
* **Ration & Polio Distribution Nodes:** The National Health Mission and Food Civil Supplies log remote hamlet names where mobile health teams drop polio vaccines or distribute flour/oil bags twice a year.

### Part 2: If Nobody Can Reach Them in Time, What Does the Platform Do?

If road access is crushed by a landslide, your platform does **not** tell civilian users to blindly wander into the wilderness. Instead, it generates actionable intelligence for two groups: **the military/rescuers**, and **the trapped villagers themselves**.

```
                           [ ROAD CUT-OFF DETECTED ]
                      (Primary Ground Corridor Severed)
                                      │
                   ┌──────────────────┴──────────────────┐
                   ▼                                     ▼
        [ FOR FIRST RESPONDERS ]              [ FOR TRAPPED POPULATIONS ]
  1. Find Automated Heli/Drone LZ       1. Compute Least-Cost Footpath (LCP)
     (Flat clearing, slope < 5°)           (Follows stable ridges, avoids valleys)
  2. Calculate 72-Hour Air Drop Tons    2. Classify: "Evacuate" vs "Shelter-in-Place"
     (Water, rations, trauma kits)         (If foot trails cross active rockfalls)
```

#### 1. Automated Detection of "Trapped / Isolated" Status

The platform runs a network reachability algorithm on OpenStreetMap (OSM) data.

* If all connected road edges to the settlement intersect a Red Zone (landslide/flood polygon), the system flags the habitation as:
**`STATUS: CRITICAL_ISOLATION | TERRESTRIAL_ACCESS: ZERO`**

#### 2. Calculating the "Safe Walking Corridor" (Least Cost Path - LCP)

Can we show people a safe path to walk out? **Yes, but under strict physical constraints.**

* **Never send people down into riverbeds or ravines:** Flash floods, mudflows, and rolling boulders concentrate in valleys.
* **The Algorithm:** The system calculates a **Least-Cost Path (LCP)** across the digital elevation model (DEM):
* **Cost = Infinity:** Through slope areas $>35^\circ$, active landslide polygons, flood inundation zones, and steep cliffs.
* **Cost = Low:** Along flat mountain ridges (*dhaars*) and pre-existing historical walking trails that sit on solid bedrock.


* **Output:** A green vector line on the dashboard marking the **Tactical Foot-Evacuation Corridor** to the nearest safe roadhead or staging camp.

#### 3. The "Shelter-in-Place" Safety Check

If the terrain surrounding the village is too dangerous (e.g., all ridgelines are crumbling or the slope below is actively sliding), guiding people to walk will get them killed.

* If every radiating path has a hazard score $>0.75$, the platform flags:
**`RECOMMENDATION: DO NOT EVACUATE ON FOOT — SHELTER IN PLACE`**
* It marks the safest high-stability open area within the village (such as a flat terraced schoolyard or plateau) as the **Internal Assembly Ground**.

#### 4. Automated Helicopter / Drone Landing Zone (LZ/DZ) Identification

If vehicles cannot reach the settlement, help must arrive by air (Indian Army/Air Force Mi-17/Cheetah helicopters or heavy-lift logistics drones). The platform automatically scans candidate coordinates within a 1 km radius of the village using elevation and land-cover data:

* **Slope Filter:** Flat terrain with slope $<5^\circ$.
* **Obstacle Clearance:** No tall trees, transmission lines, or cliff faces within a $30\text{m}\times 30\text{m}$ radius.
* **Soil Bearing:** High ground outside the flood zone.
* The system pins a blue target on the map: **`SUGGESTED HELIPAD / AIRDROP LZ`** with exact GPS coordinates and approach vector for the pilots.

#### 5. Instantaneous 72-Hour Airdrop Payload Sizing

Using the estimated building population, the tactical engine instantly calculates cargo requirements for the Air Force or NDRF:

$$\text{Daily Potable Water} = P_{\text{est}} \times 3\text{ Liters} + \text{Halogen Tablets}$$

### 1. The Spatial Coordinate & Projection Trap (CRS / EPSG)

This is where nearly all geospatial development hits an immediate roadblock: **Web Mercator vs. Real Ground Meters**.

* **The Problem:**
* MapLibre GL and CesiumJS render in **EPSG:4326** (WGS84 Latitude/Longitude in degrees) or **EPSG:3857** (Web Mercator in planar projected meters).
* If you run a query like `ST_Area(geom)` or `ST_Buffer(geom, 1000)` on an `EPSG:4326` geometry in PostGIS, **PostgreSQL treats the units as degrees, not meters**. A 1,000-degree buffer will encompass the entire Earth, causing queries to fail or return meaningless values.
* Web Mercator (`EPSG:3857`) severely distorts physical distances and areas the further you get from the equator (in the Himalayas, area distortion can exceed 15–20%).


* **The Decision Before Coding:**
* Define two coordinate systems in your backend:
1. **Storage & Display:** `EPSG:4326` (Standard WGS84 for GeoJSON/Frontend).
2. **Calculations (Area, Slope, Carrying Capacity Buffers):** Cast dynamically to `geography` type, or reproject to the local **UTM Zone** in meters:
* For Uttarakhand / Himalayas: **`EPSG:32644` (WGS 84 / UTM Zone 44N)**.
* For Wayanad / Western Ghats: **`EPSG:32643` (WGS 84 / UTM Zone 43N)**.




* **Rule in Code:**
```sql
-- WRONG (returns square degrees)
SELECT ST_Area(boundary) FROM habitations;

-- CORRECT (transforms to UTM meters, returns accurate square meters)
SELECT ST_Area(ST_Transform(boundary, 32644)) FROM habitations;

```

### 1. The 4 Macro Metrics (The Tripwires)

#### Metric 1: The Antecedent Precipitation Index (API) & Rainfall Intensity

Rainfall triggers more than 85% of non-seismic landslides and flash floods in India. However, raw 24-hour rainfall alone is deceptive: 100 mm of rain on dry, cracked summer soil is absorbed easily, but 50 mm of rain on soil that has been soaking for two weeks causes catastrophic slope liquidation.

* **The Formula to Track (Antecedent Precipitation Index):**

$$API_t = P_t + k \cdot API_{t-1}$$


* $P_t$: Rainfall on day $t$ (from IMD $0.25^\circ \times 0.25^\circ$ gridded open data or NASA GPM).
* $k$: Soil moisture decay factor (typically $0.84\text{ to }0.88$ for Himalayan/Western Ghats lithologies).
* $API_{t-1}$: Running rainfall accumulator from the previous 15 to 30 days.


* **The Trigger Rule:**
* **Alert State:** 24-hour rainfall $>115.5\text{ mm}$ (IMD "Very Heavy Rain" threshold), **OR**
* **Critical Saturation:** Cumulative 72-hour rainfall $>200\text{ mm}$ combined with an $API > 150\text{ mm}$.
* *Result:* Instantly spins up the high-resolution landslide and flood routing engine for that specific micro-watershed.



#### Metric 2: Seismic Shaking in Tectonic Zones IV & V

India is divided into four seismic zones (Zone II to Zone V). You do not need to process quakes occurring in stable interior zones (e.g., Zone II in central India).

* **The Filter:**
* Ingest the National Center for Seismology (NCS) / USGS GeoJSON feed every 60 seconds (a tiny, low-bandwidth text payload).
* **Tripwire Condition:** Any seismic event where:

$$\text{Magnitude } (M) \ge 4.2 \quad \text{AND} \quad \text{Focal Depth } \le 30\text{ km} \quad \text{AND} \quad \text{Location} \in \text{Seismic Zone IV or V}$$


* *Result:* The system computes the epicentral attenuation radius ($R = 10^{0.5M - 1}\text{ km}$) and establishes that circle as the high-resolution processing bounding box.



#### Metric 3: Central Water Commission (CWC) Hydrographs

* **The Metric:** The Central Water Commission operates telemetry gauges on every major Indian river basin, reporting water level ($H$) against two national benchmarks:
* **Warning Level ($H_w$):** River is filling active floodplains.
* **Danger Level ($H_d$):** Embankment overtopping is imminent.


* **Tripwire Condition:** When a gauge hits $(H_d - 0.5\text{m})$ and the upstream gauge rate of change $\frac{dH}{dt} > 0.3\text{ m/hour}$, create an ROI polygon 15 km downstream along the river vector.

#### Metric 4: Static Susceptibility Prior (GSI NLSM Layer)

* **The One-Time Filter:** The Geological Survey of India (GSI) has already completed the **National Landslide Susceptibility Mapping (NLSM)** across 0.42 million square kilometers of India's hilly tracts.
* You do not calculate susceptibility for the entire country from scratch. Store the GSI district classification as a static mask in PostGIS:
* Only habitations residing within GSI-designated **"High"** or **"Very High"** susceptibility grids are enrolled in the active surveillance pool (~140 districts nationwide).
* The remaining ~600 plain/plateau districts are put into sleep mode, reducing database indexing overhead by roughly 80%.

### 3. Why Fine-Tuning is the Wrong Approach

Teams often assume that "fine-tuning our own AI" earns more points. In disaster management and defense applications, fine-tuning an LLM on spatial data is actually an anti-pattern for three reasons:

1. **Catastrophic Forgetting & Schema Breakage:** Fine-tuning an instruct model often damages its native function-calling abilities and strict JSON adherence. A fine-tuned model might start outputting conversational text when you needed valid JSON to query the map.
2. **Hallucination of Static Data:** If you fine-tune a model on village populations or hazard zones, that knowledge is permanently baked into its weights. If village census numbers update or an earthquake shifts a fault line tomorrow, the model continues generating obsolete numbers from its training weights.
3. **Data Scarcity:** You do not have 10,000 paired, verified examples of disaster relocation queries and NDMA reports to run a proper supervised fine-tuning (SFT) pipeline.

---

### 4. What to Build Instead: The 3 "Zero-Fine-Tuning" Techniques

Instead of fine-tuning, production systems use three techniques that yield higher accuracy, lower development time, and verifiable outputs.

#### A. Constrained Decoding / Structured JSON Outputs

Force the 7B model to output strictly valid JSON conforming to your Pydantic schema using engine-level grammar enforcement (like Ollama’s `format: "json"` mode or Outlines/Instructor). The model is physically incapable of returning conversational filler or broken syntax:

```python

    "name": "execute_spatial_analysis",
    "parameters": {
        "habitation_name": "string",
        "action": ["ASSESS_RED_ZONE", "FIND_RELOCATION_SITES", "CALCULATE_AIRDROP"],
        "filter_reserve_forest": True
    }
}

```

#### B. In-Context Few-Shot Prompting

Provide 3 to 5 realistic input-output examples directly inside the system prompt:

```text
System: You are PUNARVAS-AI Spatial Dispatcher. Convert user intent to spatial parameters.

Example 1:
Input: "जोशीमठ सेक्टर 4 में कितना खतरा है?"
Output: {"habitation_name": "Joshimath Sector 4", "action": "ASSESS_RED_ZONE"}

Output: {"habitation_name": "Chooralmala", "action": "FIND_RELOCATION_SITES"}
```

A 7B model like Qwen 2.5 replicates this pattern with greater than 98% accuracy.

#### C. Lightweight Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

For the NDMA DPR generation module, load official NDMA guidelines (such as the *SDRF Allocation Norms* and *Landslide Rehabilitation Protocols*) into a local vector store (like ChromaDB or PostGIS `pgvector`).

* When generating the final report, retrieve the exact compensation clauses and building code requirements and inject them into the 7B model's prompt.
* The model summarizes and formats real government policy text rather than guessing from its internal memory.

### 2. The Core Mathematical Building Blocks

#### A. Hydro-Mechanical Hazard Index ($\mathcal{H}_i \in [0, 1]$)

Combines geotechnical slope failure (Iverson transient infiltration), Cruden & Varnes InSAR creep velocity, and upstream kinematic runout:

$$\mathcal{H}_i = \min\left(1.0, \; \max\left( \left[1.0 - \min_{t \le 72\text{h}} F_s(Z, t)\right], \; \frac{v_{\text{InSAR}}}{v_{\text{crit}}}, \; \mathbb{I}_{\text{runout}} \right)\right)$$

* **Transient Factor of Safety ($F_s(Z, t)$):**

$$F_s(Z, t) = \frac{\tan\phi'}{\tan\beta} + \frac{c' - \psi(Z, t)\gamma_w \tan\phi'}{\gamma_{\text{sat}} Z \sin\beta \cos\beta}$$



Where $\psi(Z, t)$ is the transient pore-water pressure from the 1D diffusion equation $\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t} = D_0 \cos^2\beta \frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial Z^2}$ forced by IMD rainfall intensity.
* **Cruden-Varnes Creep Normalizer:** $v_{\text{crit}} = 1.6\text{ m/year}$ ($1600\text{ mm/year}$).
* **Kinematic Runout Indicator ($\mathbb{I}_{\text{runout}}$):** Equal to $1.0$ if the habitation lies inside an upstream debris-flow travel-angle cone where $\tan\alpha_E \le \mu_{\text{Voellmy}}$ ($0.15\text{–}0.30$); otherwise $0.0$.

#### B. Dasymetric Exposure Index ($\mathcal{E}_i \in [0, 1]$)

Calculates the exact share of the population situated directly inside the hazard polygon:

$$\mathcal{E}_i = \min\left(1.0, \; \frac{P_{\text{affected}}}{P_{\text{benchmark}}}\right) = \min\left(1.0, \; \frac{P_{\text{village}} \times \left( \frac{\sum_{k \in \mathcal{B}_{\text{Red}}} a_k}{\sum_{m=1}^{N} a_m} \right)}{P_{\text{benchmark}}}\right)$$

Where $a_k$ is the area of individual building footprints (Google/OSM) intersecting the red polygon, and $P_{\text{benchmark}} = 1,000$ persons (standard Gram Panchayat cluster).

#### C. Structural & Demographic Vulnerability ($\mathcal{V}_i \in [0, 1]$)

Directly incorporates Census Primary Census Abstract (PCA) socio-demographic indicators:

$$\mathcal{V}_i = 0.45 \cdot \left(\frac{N_{\text{kutcha}}}{N_{\text{total}}}\right) + 0.35 \cdot \left(\frac{P_{\text{sc\_st}} + P_{\text{elderly}} + P_{\text{infants}}}{P_{\text{affected}}}\right) + 0.20 \cdot \left(1.0 - \mathcal{A}_{\text{access}}\right)$$

Where $\mathcal{A}_{\text{access}} \in [0, 1]$ is the OpenStreetMap topological graph edge-connectivity score.

#### D. Multi-Vector Carrying Capacity Matrix ($C_{ij} \in [0, 1]$)

Measures the capacity of candidate parcel $j$ to sustainably absorb displaced habitation $i$:

$$C_{ij} = \min\left(1.0, \; 0.30 \cdot \left[\frac{A_{\text{usable}}(j)}{P_i \times 12\text{ m}^2}\right] + 0.35 \cdot \left[\frac{Q_{\text{dry\_baseflow}}(j)}{P_i \times 55\text{ LPCD}}\right] + 0.20 \cdot I(j) + 0.15 \cdot \mathcal{M}_{\text{livelihood}}(i, j)\right)$$

* **Land Space Requirement:** Evaluated against the MoHUA URDPFI statutory standard of $12\text{ m}^2/\text{person}$.
* **Hydrological Baseflow:** Evaluated against the Jal Jeevan Mission rural statutory standard of $55\text{ LPCD}$ using lean-season (March–May) aquifer discharge $Q_{\text{dry\_baseflow}}$.
* **Infrastructure Proximity ($I(j)$):** Distance decay function to PMGSY paved roads, electricity lines, and Primary Health Centers.
* **Livelihood Matching ($\mathcal{M}_{\text{livelihood}}$):** Cosine similarity between habitation occupational vector $\vec{L}_i$ (agriculture, pastoralism, commerce) and parcel resource availability vector $\vec{R}_j$.

#### E. Cadastral Integrity & Legal Encumbrance Multiplier ($\Lambda_j \in [0, 1]$)

Catches the "paper vs. ground" discrepancy:

$$\Lambda_j = \left(1.0 - \delta_{\text{FRA}}\right) \times \left(1.0 - \min\left(1.0, \frac{\text{NDBI\_Built\_Area}_{\text{satellite}}}{\text{Cadastral\_Vacant\_Area}_{\text{RoR}}}\right)\right) \times \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta_{\text{grid\_shift}}}{100\text{ m}}\right)$$

* $\delta_{\text{FRA}} = 1.0$ if an active Forest Rights Act (FRA 2006) community claim or Reserve Forest boundary exists; otherwise $0.0$.


* **NDBI Check:** Rejects paper "vacant" government land if Sentinel-2 / high-res optical imagery reveals unrecorded settlement structures.


* $\Delta_{\text{grid\_shift}}$: Polynomial coordinate divergence (in meters) between digitized British-era chain surveys (Bhulekh) and WGS84 ground control points.



#### F. Institutional Latency & Governance Escalator ($\Theta_i \ge 1.0$)

### 5. Final Allocation Execution in Python (`scipy.optimize`)

At the platform's final processing stage, compute the pairwise cost matrix $C[i, j] = 100.0 - \Omega_{ij}$ and execute the **Modified Hungarian Linear Sum Assignment Algorithm** to pair each vulnerable village $i$ to its optimal, unencumbered candidate parcel $j$ while maximizing total systemic safety:

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.optimize import linear_sum_assignment

def solve_optimal_resettlement(omega_matrix: np.ndarray):
    """
    omega_matrix: 2D array of size (N_habitations, M_candidate_sites)
                  containing computed Omega_ij scores [0 to 100].
    Maximizes overall relocation viability while enforcing 1-to-1 site allocation.
    """
    # Convert maximization problem to cost minimization
    cost_matrix = 100.0 - omega_matrix
    
    # Run Kuhn-Munkres (Hungarian) algorithm: O(N^3)
    habitation_ind, site_ind = linear_sum_assignment(cost_matrix)
    
    allocations = []
    for h, s in zip(habitation_ind, site_ind):
        score = omega_matrix[h, s]
        if score > 0.0:  # Must have cleared legal & capacity constraints
            allocations.append({
                "habitation_index": int(h),
                "allocated_site_index": int(s),
                "master_index_score": round(float(score), 2),
                "action_tier": "TIER_1_IMMEDIATE" if score >= 75 else ("TIER_2_SHORT_TERM" if score >= 50 else "TIER_3_MONITOR")
            })
    return allocations
```

### Pillar 3: Physical & Terrain Safety (Geomechanics)

A candidate parcel must be geotechnically incapable of experiencing the same disaster that displaced the original village:

1. **Topographical Usability:** Contiguous slope angle $\beta < 15^\circ$. Slopes steeper than $15^\circ$ require expensive civil terracing and are vulnerable to induced cut-slope failures.
2. **Kinematic Runout Exclusion (The Wayanad Check):** The parcel must sit entirely outside any upstream debris-flow travel-angle cone ($\tan\alpha_E \le 0.20$). Even if the parcel itself is flat, if an unstable $40^\circ$ mountain ridge looms 2 km above it, the parcel is disqualified.


3. **Subsurface Bedrock Integrity (The Joshimath Check):** Cross-referenced with Geological Survey of India (GSI) lithology. The parcel must sit on stable bedrock (granite, gneiss, compact sandstone), not unconsolidated glacial moraines or ancient landslide debris scree.
